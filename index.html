Approx. 55 min · official access only
St. Louis Cemetery No. 1

Reserve an early official tour so the cemetery does not consume the middle of the day. Check in 15 minutes early at Basin St. Station, 501 Basin St. The cemetery is not open for independent visits.

Why this matters

The above-ground tombs are not just visual shorthand for New Orleans. Use the tour to understand burial practice, Catholic institutions, race and status, preservation, and the way a “city of the dead” became part of the city’s public identity.

Stan note

The operator says wheelchairs and scooters are allowed and the pathway is maneuverable, but the tour is still about 55 minutes. This is easiest as a Laura + Peter activity unless Stan has mobility support and wants to come.

Upriver: Americans, Architecture + a City Made Buildable

Follow New Orleans beyond the Creole core into the American city, then use the afternoon to connect growth with infrastructure and preservation before dinner with Stan and his friends.

Approx.
9:00 AM
Paid / flexibleLaura + Peter
Transit as part of the experience
St. Charles Streetcar → Garden District

Leave enough time to ride the streetcar rather than defaulting to a car. Use live RTA/Le Pass information that morning and the tour confirmation for the exact meeting point.

Interpretive lens

You are moving upriver from the old Creole city toward districts shaped by American newcomers after the Louisiana Purchase. Treat the journey itself as part of the historical sequence.

10:00 AM
– noon
Paid / reserveLaura + Peter
$40/person · 2 hours · daily 10 AM + 1 PM
Garden District Architecture Walking Tour

Book the 10:00 AM NOLA Tours walk. Its stated framing is exactly the story you want: American newcomers building upriver from a Creole city that did not readily absorb them, expressed through Greek Revival, Italianate, Queen Anne and other residential architecture.

What to ask / notice

Push beyond “beautiful houses.” Ask about the former City of Lafayette, annexation, wealth and slavery, lot patterns, domestic service, architectural fashion, and how the Garden District related to the older Creole city.

Preferred if confirmed — HNOC “The Pumps That Sank New Orleans”

The Historic New Orleans Collection offers a free ~20-minute Shortcut Talk at 2:00 PM about the pumping and sewer technologies that enabled New Orleans to expand while contributing to subsidence. This is almost perfectly aligned with your interest in infrastructure and city development.

Catch: the 2:00 PM talks rotate. Contact HNOC before the trip or ask that morning whether this topic is running. If it is not, do not reorganize the day around it.
